# Prospective Provider Outreach Shortlist

> **Status: public-source screening list; no authorization to engage.** The organizations below are research candidates, not endorsements, recommendations, due-diligence approvals, or proposed appointments. No organization has been contacted, screened for availability, given non-public information, selected, retained, paid, or asked to quote. The user must explicitly approve each named recipient, the final RFI, and the permitted disclosure set before any outreach.

## 1. Purpose and decision boundary

This list translates the provider-neutral engagement framework into a limited set of organizations whose public materials describe work relevant to the required mainnet-readiness workstreams. It does **not** establish regulatory eligibility, technical suitability, independence, pricing, capacity, or the availability of any service. Those questions require conflict checks, written proposals, and specialist review.

The governing product boundary is unchanged: the reference node remains a no-value public testnet, and BONDS must not be represented as live, USD-pegged, redeemable, collateralized, insured, or investable. The OCC’s proposed rules describe payment-stablecoin issuer requirements across areas including reserve assets, redemption, risk management, audits, custody, and capital/operational backstops; FinCEN’s proposal addresses AML/CFT and sanctions programs for permitted payment stablecoin issuers.[1] [2]

## 3. Mandatory screening controls

The matrix is deliberately not ranked. The program must apply the same threshold questions to each candidate: written conflicts, exact legal entity, named accountable professionals, jurisdictional fit, scope and exclusions, relevant evidence, data-handling controls, professional insurance where applicable, incident escalation, fee structure, and termination rights. A candidate that cannot disclose a material conflict, identify the contracting entity, or scope its deliverable against G-01 through G-12 cannot advance to outreach approval.

Financial assurance and reserve evidence must be assessed for scope, liabilities, controls, and ongoing availability—not merely a point-in-time asset representation. The PCAOB’s investor advisory cautions that proof-of-reserve reports are inherently limited and are not equivalent to audits.[18] For retirement work, the FINRA investor alert cautions that self-directed IRA custodians generally administer assets rather than evaluate investments or verify promoter information.[19]

## 5. Recommended sequencing if the user authorizes outreach

The first RFI wave should be limited to **one or two counsel candidates** and **one custody candidate**, because legal model and permitted operating structure determine the meaning of the reserve, redemption, AML, IRA, and audit scopes. A second, parallel research or RFI wave may cover one compliance-control candidate and one independent security assessor. IRA and financial-assurance outreach should occur only after counsel has clarified the entity, jurisdiction, and the controls to be examined.

This sequencing does not authorize any commitment. It reduces the risk of receiving proposals for an operating model that has not yet been permitted or designed.
