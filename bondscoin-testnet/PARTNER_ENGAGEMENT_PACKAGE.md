# Bonds Coin Partner Engagement Package

> **Status: provider-neutral diligence package.** This document does not select, recommend, retain, contact, or authorize any external provider. Any engagement, fee commitment, data disclosure, contract, reserve arrangement, custody relationship, or audit appointment requires separate user approval and qualified professional review.

## 1. Objective and order of operations

The Bonds Coin mainnet-readiness program cannot be completed through protocol code alone. Before any value-bearing launch decision, the program needs independent legal, reserve, custody, financial-control, compliance, and security evidence. The right engagement order is therefore to establish the legal/issuer and operating model first, confirm the reserve/custody and redemption design second, and only then scope independent financial and technical audits against a stable architecture.

The OCC’s 2026 proposed GENIUS Act rulemaking describes requirements concerning payment-stablecoin issuer activities, reserve assets, redemption, risk management, audits, reports, custody, applications, supervision, and capital/operational backstops.[1] FinCEN’s April 2026 proposal describes AML/CFT and sanctions-compliance requirements for permitted payment stablecoin issuers.[2] The engagement package is organized around those external evidence needs, not around an assumption that a BONDS mainnet is approved or should be activated.

| Sequence | Workstream | Purpose | Permitted action at this stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Financial-services and digital-asset counsel | Assess issuer structure, jurisdictions, token classification, contracts, marketing, governance, and regulatory pathway | Execute a conflict check and request a written scope proposal only |
| 2 | Reserve, treasury, and qualified-custody design | Define eligible reserve policy, segregation, reconciliation, redemption, and key/custody boundaries | Request capability, jurisdiction, and control evidence only |
| 3 | AML/CFT, sanctions, privacy, tax, and IRA specialists | Convert product requirements into accountable operational controls | Request design review and implementation-scope proposals only |
| 4 | Independent financial assurance | Scope reserve, reconciliation, financial-reporting, and attestation procedures after the operating model is fixed | Request independence and methodology information only |
| 5 | Independent security assessment | Scope protocol, cryptography, P2P, storage, custody, web/API, cloud, and incident-response reviews | Request qualifications, methodology, and conflict information only |
| 6 | Launch governance | Review all evidence and make a documented go/no-go decision | No launch authorization until every mainnet gate is green |

## 2. Partner workstreams and minimum credentials

| Workstream | Minimum qualifications to verify | Deliverables required before any activation decision | Conflicts to disclose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regulatory counsel | Relevant financial-services, payments, digital-asset, sanctions, and consumer-protection capability in target jurisdictions | Written classification and authorization analysis; regulatory pathway; contract/disclosure review; marketing restrictions | Issuer, exchange, custodian, reserve manager, competitor, or regulator relationships |
| Reserve and treasury operator | Ability to support segregated eligible assets, reconciliation, redemption liquidity, reporting, and incident escalation | Reserve policy; control map; reconciliation cadence; outage/failure procedures; reporting interface | Compensation tied to reserve yield, issuance volume, or affiliated trading |
| Qualified custody provider | Applicable custody authority, asset segregation, key controls, incident response, audit evidence, and support operations | Custody architecture; key and recovery controls; service-level commitments; audit artifacts | Co-mingling, affiliate lending, rehypothecation, or conflicts with treasury operator |
| AML/CFT and sanctions specialist | Program-design experience applicable to financial institutions and relevant payment stablecoin requirements | Risk assessment; onboarding/monitoring design; escalation, recordkeeping, training, and independent-testing plan | Surveillance vendor incentives, data-sharing conflicts, or fee structures tied to false-positive volume |
| IRA custodian and tax specialist | Qualified retirement-account operations, reporting, distribution, rollover, and prohibited-transaction capability | IRA operating model; account-administration matrix; tax/reporting plan; disclosure review | Promotion of unsupported “IRS approved” claims or product-compensation conflicts |
| Independent financial assurance firm | Independence, relevant financial-controls methodology, quality controls, and ability to assess reserve/reconciliation evidence | Engagement scope; independence letter; evidence list; reporting and remediation process | Audit and operating-management roles, contingent compensation, or reserve-management affiliation |
| Independent security assessor | Demonstrated protocol, cryptography, P2P, storage, wallet/custody, cloud, and incident-response assessment capability | Audit scope; threat model; test methodology; findings format; retest commitment; disclosure policy | Prior implementation role, token allocation, contingent compensation, or undisclosed affiliate role |

## 3. Provider-neutral evaluation rubric

No provider should be retained solely on brand recognition, sales claims, token exposure, or a promised launch timeline. The selection committee should score evidence, independence, scope fit, and operational accountability.

Where the final assurance scope requires a PCAOB-registered public-accounting firm, the engagement assessment must additionally account for applicable PCAOB ethics and independence rules, including auditor-independence and contingent-fee safeguards.[3] This does not predetermine the type of assurance engagement or imply that the program is eligible for an audit; qualified counsel and the engagement firm must determine the applicable standard.

| Criterion | Weight | Required evidence | Disqualifying signal |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Independence and conflicts | 20% | Written conflict disclosure, ownership/affiliate map, fee structure | Contingent fee tied to issuance, reserves, token price, or audit outcome |
| Relevant capability | 20% | Comparable regulated/payments, custody, audit, or protocol work; named accountable leads | Generic capability claim with no verifiable relevant experience |
| Scope completeness | 15% | Written mapping to G-01 through G-12, assumptions, exclusions, and dependencies | Scope excludes the highest-risk controls without a replacement plan |
| Evidence quality | 15% | Sample deliverable outline, methodology, quality controls, review process | Reliance on unverifiable marketing or oral assurances |
| Security and data handling | 10% | Data classification, access controls, secure collaboration, retention and deletion policy | Request for unrestricted production data or keys before engagement controls exist |
| Operating resilience | 10% | Named escalation path, continuity, incident response, capacity, and service levels | No accountable incident process or key-person dependency with no backup |
| Commercial clarity | 10% | Fixed/phase-based scope, change control, fees, termination, insurance, and ownership terms | Hidden fees, unclear deliverables, or pressure for immediate commitment |

The evaluation committee should retain the scoring sheet, source documents, conflict disclosures, and decision record. A score is a diligence aid, not a substitute for professional judgment or legal review.

## 4. Request-for-information template

The following RFI is deliberately written to seek facts rather than a sales commitment. It should be issued only after the user approves the recipient list and the information-sharing terms.

> **Subject:** Request for Information — Bonds Coin Mainnet Readiness Workstream
>
> Bonds Bank is conducting provider-neutral diligence for a prospective, value-bearing digital-asset program. The current technology is a no-value testnet reference; no mainnet activation, token issuance, customer custody, reserve claim, redemption, or trading function has been authorized. We request information solely to assess fit, independence, scope, and evidence requirements.
>
> Please provide: (1) your relevant qualifications and jurisdictions; (2) conflicts, affiliates, and compensation structure; (3) proposed work scope and exclusions mapped to the attached workstream; (4) methodology and quality-control process; (5) sample deliverable outline; (6) named accountable engagement leads; (7) data-security and retention practices; (8) expected dependencies and client inputs; (9) fee structure and change-control approach; and (10) your availability for a non-binding scoping discussion.
>
> Do not request private keys, customer personal information, reserve funds, or production credentials. Any later engagement is subject to separate approval, conflict review, contractual terms, and applicable professional oversight.

## 5. Workstream-specific questions

| Workstream | Questions that must be answered before shortlisting |
|---|---|
| Counsel | Which issuer models and jurisdictions will be assessed? Which product, marketing, reserve, redemption, and governance documents are in scope? What conclusions require separate specialist opinions? |
| Reserve/custody | How are assets segregated, reconciled, accessed, and reported? What happens during a bank, custodian, feed, or redemption outage? Can the provider supply independently reviewable controls? |
| AML/CFT and sanctions | How will customer risk, screening, monitoring, escalation, reporting, training, and independent testing be governed? What data flows and privacy constraints are expected? |
| IRA/tax | Which entity is the custodian of record? How are contributions, rollovers, distributions, recordkeeping, tax reporting, and prohibited transactions administered? |
| Financial assurance | How is independence established? Which reserve, reconciliation, and reporting evidence can be tested? How are exceptions, remediation, and retests reported? |
| Security assessment | Which protocol and operational layers are in scope? What cryptographic-review, fuzzing, network-abuse, cloud, wallet, and incident-response methods will be used? How will findings be ranked and retested? |

## 6. Information-sharing and engagement controls

Before any external information is shared, the program must assign a data owner, classify the material, confirm a minimum-necessary disclosure set, and use an approved secure collaboration method. The public testnet source, threat model, and mainnet-readiness package may be shared as public materials. Private keys, customer data, reserve details, prospective counterparty information, security findings, or production credentials must not be shared through an RFI.

Provider contracts, when separately approved, should include scope, independence, confidentiality, data handling, work-product ownership, limitation of access, disclosure rights, insurance, escalation, incident notification, termination, and remediation/retest terms. Counsel must review agreements that could create regulatory, custody, reserve, or customer-protection obligations.

## 7. Decisions that require explicit user approval

The following actions are outside this preparation package and require a specific user decision before any execution:

1. Selecting, retaining, paying, or signing with a provider.
2. Sharing non-public technical, customer, reserve, custody, security, or financial information.
3. Opening any reserve, custody, bank, wallet, issuer, trading, or IRA account.
4. Authorizing an audit scope, publishing a security finding, or accepting a remediation risk.
5. Activating any value-bearing network, token issuance, custody, reserve claim, redemption, trading, or customer-facing financial feature.
